# Resolve: a routing failure changed the design

Independent work sample by Elle Helvig. Updated 13 November 2026. All examples and test data are synthetic.

## Business problem

A People Operations workflow can identify an HR topic and still miss the reason an employee needs specialist help. A routine approval queue cannot substitute for Employee Relations or Legal intake.

## My contribution

I framed the HR workflow, defined data and decision boundaries, designed the evaluation and release criteria, and built the prototype with AI-assisted development. The browser experience uses rules and fixed responses. An optional model screen is a separate routing experiment.

## What the evidence changed

The original rules passed 60 familiar development cases and failed every complete pass condition in 16 developer-authored paraphrases. Five specialist concerns were missed. I retained the failures and withheld production use. Historical model-screen recordings suggest a routing benefit alongside false refusals, but are neither independent validation nor evidence for the current prompt.

The 13 November correction narrows a false injection trigger for benign bypass wording and routes explicit workplace religion-targeting language to ER before employee lookup. Paired regression controls preserve routine requests and refusal boundaries. The rules now pass 1/16 known paraphrases; four specialist misses remain. This targeted fix does not establish generalization.

## Decision and next evidence

Keep the synthetic demonstration and production hold. Before reconsidering routing, obtain an independently authored private case set, adjudicated specialist labels, and repeated controlled model comparisons. Then run a small synthetic practitioner usability pilot measuring total work, quality, review burden, and trust.

No employee deployment, participant study, time saving, adoption outcome, or cost benefit has been measured. Simulated reviewer names and temporary history do not establish authorization or protected audit records.
